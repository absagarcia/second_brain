# Dinero: domina el juego (MONEY Master the Game: 7 Simple Steps to Financial Freedom)

Widely known to be by Tony Robbins (2014), built on interviews with
prominent investors — same caveat as the other book ingests: not stated in
the raw file itself (a Kindle highlights export, no metadata page), so
this attribution is recalled general knowledge, not source-traceable.

**Nature of this source**: personal Kindle highlights export — same
double-compression caveat as the other book pages in this wiki. This is
the longest/densest highlight set ingested so far, spanning all 7 parts of
the book.

## Structure (per the book's own 7 parts)

1. **La decisión** — take control of your money/life; the framing
   question set from the intro: what do you really want (vision), what
   matters (values), how do you get there (method), what's stopping you
   (obstacles), how will you know you got there (measurement).
2. **Las reglas del juego** — debunking financial-industry myths (can't
   reliably beat the market; target-date funds "invest and forget"; the
   lies we tell ourselves about money).
3. **¿Cuánto cuestan nuestros sueños?** — the core mechanic: **"ganar más,
   gastar menos y automatizar el ahorro"** (earn more, spend less,
   automate savings) is called "the great secret." Time is framed as the
   most valuable resource, more than money itself.
4. **La decisión de inversión más importante** — asset allocation via
   three "buckets": see [[investment-bucket-strategy]].
5. **Ganar sin perder** — building a lifetime income plan; income framed
   as freedom.
6. **Invertir como el 0,001%** — wisdom collected from interviews with
   billionaire investors: Ray Dalio (referenced via an anecdote, not
   detailed), Paul Tudor Jones ("always follow the dominant trend, never
   trade against it"; never invest below the 200-day moving average),
   Mary Callahan Erdoes ("financial stress never fully goes away
   regardless of wealth/success level"), Kyle Bass (ambition as the common
   trait among high achievers, beyond credentials or talent), Marc Faber
   ("the best time to buy is when there's blood in the streets").
7. **Haz, disfruta y comparte** — closes on fulfillment vs. success
   without fulfillment being framed as "the greatest failure," plus a
   brief note on healthy vs. unhealthy ways people process anger/frustration
   (repress it / seek revenge vs. channel it into exercise).

## Contrarian-timing aphorisms (Part 4, market timing chapter)

- "Las mejores oportunidades se presentan en tiempos de máximo pesimismo."
- "Sé temeroso cuando los demás son avariciosos, y avaricioso cuando los
  demás son temerosos" (commonly attributed to Warren Buffett; the
  excerpt doesn't attribute it explicitly).
- "El mejor momento para comprar es cuando hay sangre en las calles"
  (commonly attributed to Baron Rothschild; likewise not attributed in the
  excerpt itself).
- A constant/automated investment plan is framed as a way to make market
  volatility work for you rather than against you (dollar-cost averaging,
  though not named as such in the excerpt).

## Relevance to this wiki

The "earn more, spend less, automate savings" principle directly
reinforces [[habitos-para-ser-millonario]]'s frugality rule from an
independent source, and the "automate" part is a concrete financial
instance of [[habitos-atomicos]]'s systems-over-goals thesis (see
[[goal-setting-vs-systems-based-habits]]) — automating savings removes
reliance on daily willpower/goal-repetition entirely. The investment-
bucket framework is the most reusable piece for the `finance` domain
generally, tagged separately as [[investment-bucket-strategy]]. The
automation point itself is consolidated alongside five other independent
sources in [[systems-over-willpower]]. Its "enough-to-be-free as the real
finish line" framing feeds [[enough-hedonic-adaptation]] (added 2026-07-23).
