## What the highlighted passages cover

- **Small, empowered teams over large ones to be managed**: "product
  creation isn't about having a big team to manage; it's about having a
  small team with the right people in it."
- **Leaders as designers**: leaders who can design products, define
  architectures, and articulate exactly what needs to be delivered are
  described as the powerful ones in a digital company — a shift away from
  traditional oversight-from-a-distance management.
- **Principles as pattern-matching**: "principles are a way of seeing
  things so everything looks like 'another one of these,' and when
  another one shows up, how do I successfully handle it?"
- **Long-term thinking, decisiveness**: "the only thing I'd regret is
  never having tried" and "classic Bezos: think long term," in the
  context of faster/better decision-making.
- **Ban PowerPoint, write with clarity**: keep writing things out on paper
  with clarity and simplicity, and hold rigorous debates, as core
  attributes — an allusion to Amazon's well-known practice of narrative
  memos instead of slide decks (not elaborated further in the excerpt).
- **FAQs as anticipation**: writing FAQs as a way of anticipating key
  questions about your product ahead of time.
- **Cash over percentages**: "percentages don't pay the electric bill,
  cash does" — in a chapter on free cash flow and accounting.

## Relevance to this wiki

Tagged `athletix` as general leadership/culture reading relevant to
[[athletix-ai]]'s own team structure, though nothing here is asserted as
something the team has adopted. The "small team, high-ownership" idea and
"leaders as designers" both echo (independently) the kind of
founder-led, hands-on team structure ATHLETIX AI's pitch deck describes.
