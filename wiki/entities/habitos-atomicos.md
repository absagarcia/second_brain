# Hábitos atómicos (Atomic Habits)

Widely known to be by James Clear (2018) — same caveat as the other book
ingests: not stated in the raw file itself (a Kindle highlights export,
no metadata page), so this attribution is recalled general knowledge, not
source-traceable.

**Nature of this source**: personal Kindle highlights export — same
double-compression caveat as the other book pages in this wiki.

## Core mechanics covered in the highlights

- **Systems over goals** — the book's most reusable idea; contrasts
  directly with another book already in this wiki, so it's covered in the
  comparison page [[goal-setting-vs-systems-based-habits]] rather than
  repeated here.
- **The habit loop and Four Laws of Behavior Change** (cue → craving →
  response → reward; make it obvious/attractive/easy/satisfying) and
  **identity-based habits** (habits as votes for the type of person you
  want to become) — extracted together as
  [[four-laws-of-behavior-change]], since the book treats identity change
  as the mechanism that makes the four laws stick.
- **Premack's Principle** (cited to psychologist David Premack): "more
  probable behaviors will reinforce less probable behaviors" — the basis
  for habit-stacking/temptation-bundling tactics (e.g. pairing a wanted
  habit like gratitude journaling with an existing habit like morning
  coffee).
- **Implementation intentions**: pre-committing to a specific cue
  ("after X, I will do Y") is cited as increasing adherence to habits like
  recycling, studying, sleeping earlier, quitting smoking.
- **Self-awareness as a performance bottleneck**: many performance
  failures attributed to lack of self-awareness of one's own current
  habits/cues, before any strategy is even applied.
- Passing mention of Michael Phelps (most decorated Olympic athlete ever)
  in the "talent" chapter — genes-vs-habits framing, no further detail
  given in the excerpt.
