## The CODE method

Extracted as its own concept, see [[code-method-second-brain]] — Capture,

## Other ideas in the highlighted text

- **Capture criteria #1: "does it inspire me?"** — a feeling-based filter
  for what's worth keeping, explicitly contrasted with fact-lookup ("you
  can Google the answer to a question, but you can't Google a feeling").
- **"More is not better when it comes to thinking and creating"** (Ch. 6,
  Distill) — direct echo of [[esencialismo]]'s "menos pero mejor," from an
  independent source in a different genre (knowledge management vs.
  general life prioritization).
- **"We only know what we make"** (Ch. 7, Express) — learning/
  understanding framed as a byproduct of creating/expressing, not of
  passive consumption or capture.
- **"I never run the risk of starting something I can't finish"** — tied
  to the same Express chapter, on breaking work into small expressed
  units rather than large unstarted projects.
- **"Creative products are always shiny and new; the creative process is
  ancient and unchanging"** (Ch. 8) — a claim that the underlying process
  (not the tools) is what matters and persists.
- **"Being organized is a habit... not a personality trait you're born
  with"** (Ch. 9) — directly parallel to [[four-laws-of-behavior-change]]'s
  identity-based habits framing: organization as something built through
  repeated behavior, not an innate trait or a tool-selection problem.
  Consolidated alongside five other independent sources in
  [[systems-over-willpower]].

## Relevance to this wiki

This book is about the exact kind of system this wiki is: an external,
curated knowledge base meant to compound over time. [[daniel]] (a friend
who independently built his own second-brain system, per
[[second-brain-epistemic-design]]) is the natural cross-reference here —
nothing indicates Daniel used this specific book, so this is not claimed
as his source, just as the same genre of idea.
