# De qué hablo cuando hablo de correr

Haruki Murakami's running memoir (recalled attribution, not in the raw file).
Half about long-distance running, half about the discipline of a writing life —
the two treated as the same practice. **Long-lived** content: it's reflection,
not data.

## Load-bearing ideas

- **"Pain is inevitable. Suffering is optional."** The mantra of the book. Pain
  is a fact of endurance; the suffering you add on top is a choice. Directly
  echoed by [[generacion-dopamina]] (sitting with discomfort) and
  [[haz-cosas-dificiles]] (respond, don't react to sensation).
- **Compete with the you of yesterday.** "Si hay un contrincante al que debes
  vencer en una carrera de larga distancia, ése no es otro que el tú de ayer."
  An internal, not comparative, standard of progress — consolidated under
  [[internal-scorecard]] with [[atrevete-a-no-gustar]],
  [[mindset-la-actitud-del-exito]], and [[haz-cosas-dificiles]]. One of the
  strongest recurring themes in this batch.
- **The daily run as a mental-health ritual.** "Correr una hora todos los días,
  asegurándome... un tiempo de silencio sólo para mí, se convirtió en un hábito
  decisivo para mi salud mental." A concrete instance of [[systems-over-willpower]]
  and of the "time alone with your thoughts" prescription in
  [[minimalismo-digital]] and [[haz-cosas-dificiles]].
- **Build a system of priorities before a certain age**, or life "acaba
  volviéndose monótona y carente de eje." Convergent with
  [[esencialismo]] / [[deep-work]] on protecting an axis of attention.
- **Talent is involuntary; effort and endurance are trainable.** "El talento no
  tiene nada que ver con la voluntad. Brota... cuando quiere." Muscle is hard to
  gain and easy to lose; fat the reverse. A [[growth-vs-fixed-mindset]]-adjacent
  view: you can't summon talent, but you can show up.
