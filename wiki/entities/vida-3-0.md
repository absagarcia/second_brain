# Vida 3.0 (Life 3.0)

Widely known to be by Max Tegmark (MIT physicist, Life 3.0: Being Human in
the Age of Artificial Intelligence, 2017) — same caveat as the other book
ingests: authorship isn't stated in the raw file itself, which is a sparse
Kindle highlights export (only ~8 highlights across the whole book, no
metadata page). This attribution is recalled general knowledge, not
source-traceable.

**Nature of this source**: extremely sparse compression — the user
highlighted very little of this book relative to [[how-to-build-a-billion-dollar-app]]
and [[creando-unicornios]]. Treat this page as a few scattered pointers,
not a summary of the book's actual argument (which is much broader — the
Life 1.0/2.0/3.0 framework itself isn't even present in the highlighted
text, so it isn't described here).

## What the highlighted passages actually cover

- **Definition of intelligence**: "capacidad de alcanzar objetivos
  complejos" (ability to achieve complex goals) — extracted as its own
  concept, see [[intelligence-as-goal-achieving-capacity]]; compared
  against Harari's definition in
  [[intelligence-vs-consciousness-tegmark-harari]].
- **Brain-as-hardware framing**: synapses store roughly 100 terabytes of
  information; the brain has roughly as many neurons (~100 billion) as
  there are stars in our galaxy — used to make brain capacity
  intuitive/comparable to computing hardware.
- **Goal stability example**: the Catholic Church cited as the most
  durable organization in history (~2 millennia), critiqued for having
  goals that are simultaneously too rigid and too unstable — used
  (in context, chapter 5, "after the explosion / next ten thousand years")
  as an analogy for how a superintelligent AI's goal specification might
  need to balance stability and adaptability.
- **Thermodynamics/goals**: life maintains or increases its own complexity
  by increasing entropy (disorder) in its environment — framed as life's
  "intrinsic" thermodynamic objective, in a chapter literally titled
  "Objetivos" (Goals).
- **Consciousness/meaning**: "No es el universo el que da sentido a los
  seres conscientes, sino los seres conscientes los que dan sentido al
  universo" (it's not the universe that gives meaning to conscious beings,
  but conscious beings that give meaning to the universe) — Tegmark's
  stance that meaning is generated by consciousness, not intrinsic to
  physics.
- **Voltaire quote (1759)** on work keeping away boredom, vice, and need —
  used in the chapter on near-future job/labor impacts of AI.
- Passing mention of Eliezer Yudkowsky (AI-safety researcher, MIRI
  founder) with no further context given in the highlighted text.
