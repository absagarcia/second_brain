# Mindset, La actitud del éxito (Mindset: The New Psychology of Success)

Widely known to be by Carol Dweck (2006) — same caveat as the other book
ingests: not stated in the raw file itself (a Kindle highlights export,
no metadata page), so this attribution is recalled general knowledge, not
source-traceable.

**Nature of this source**: personal Kindle highlights export — same
double-compression caveat as the other book pages in this wiki. Coverage
spans nearly the whole book (intro through the parenting/coaching and
"changing mindsets" chapters), denser than most other book ingests here.

## Core thesis

Extracted as its own concept, see [[growth-vs-fixed-mindset]] — the
central fixed-vs-growth mindset distinction, its behavioral consequences,
and how it's taught/changed.

## Other notable material in the highlights

- **Sternberg citation**: the most important factor in acquiring
  expertise is "not any innate ability, but sustained commitment" —
  echoed later in the book's own thesis, and comparable to
  [[four-laws-of-behavior-change]]'s "durable change requires repeated
  small actions," though from a different angle (belief about ability vs.
  identity-through-habit).
- **On willpower**: "willpower is not something you either have or don't
  have — willpower needs help" (Ch. on mindset and willpower) — a
  direct, independent echo of the "environment/systems over raw
  willpower" theme already recurring in this wiki; consolidated in
  [[systems-over-willpower]] alongside five other independent sources.
- **On plans**: concrete "when, where, and how" plans (not just "what")
  are cited as driving much higher follow-through — comparable to
  [[four-laws-of-behavior-change]]'s implementation intentions
  ("after X, I will do Y"), from an independent source.
- **On maintaining change**: "once a problem improves, people often stop
  doing what caused the improvement" — a relapse-risk warning not
  present in this wiki's other habit-formation pages.
- Anecdotes across domains (sports, business leadership, romantic
  relationships, parenting/coaching) applying the same fixed/growth
  distinction — not itemized individually here since they all restate the
  same core mechanism.
