# Máximo rendimiento (Peak Performance)

**Brad Stulberg & Steve Magness**, ~2017 (Spanish edition). Unlike most book
pages in this wiki, the **authors are stated in the raw file itself** — no
recalled attribution needed. Same double-compression caveat as the rest: the
source is a Kindle highlights export, so what's here is the user's selection
*of* the book, further compressed by me.

Same Steve Magness as [[haz-cosas-dificiles]] (2022) — this is the **earlier**
book, and the two are best read as one arc: *Máximo rendimiento* builds the
load/recovery architecture, *Haz cosas difíciles* later reworks what "toughness"
means inside it. Where they overlap (stress as challenge not threat, meaning as
the thing that lets you endure), that's the **same author restating himself**,
not two independent sources — noted explicitly so this wiki doesn't
double-count it as convergence.

**Long-lived** (principles, physiology, routines). The only short-lived bits are
the company anecdotes (Google/Apple nap rooms, Zuckerberg's t-shirt Q&A from
2014) and the specific percentages.

## The spine: esfuerzo + descanso = crecimiento

The whole book is one equation, restated at every scale (a work block, a day, a
week, a year): **stress + rest = growth**. "El esfuerzo pide descanso, y el
descanso hace posible el esfuerzo." Full treatment in
[[stress-rest-growth-equation]].

Three parts:

1. **La ecuación del crecimiento** — how to load (just-manageable challenges,
   deep single-tasking, 50–90 min blocks) and how to unload (breaks, walking,
   meditation, social recovery, sleep, days off).
2. **La preparación** — routines, a space of your own, chronotype, and the people
   around you as the machinery that *expresses* the talent the equation builds:
   "si esfuerzo + descanso = crecimiento es la base sobre la que se desarrolla
   nuestro talento, nuestros hábitos y nuestro entorno nos ayudan a expresar
   plenamente ese talento."
3. **El propósito** — purpose as the lever that moves the perceived-effort
   ceiling. See [[purpose-as-performance-amplifier]].

## What's distinctive vs. what this wiki already had

**New here:**

- **Rest is reframed as productive work, not the absence of work.** Triathlon
  coach Matt Dixon's answer to what separates the best: *el descanso*. His
  athletes stop seeing rest as "no entrenamiento." Sleep gets the sharpest
  version: "No es que duerman porque sean la élite. Son la élite porque duermen."
  Hours 7–9 carry the most REM — exactly the hours most people cut.
- **Burnout is framed as a dosing failure, not a character failure.** 30–40% of
  college/amateur athletes have hit overtraining syndrome at least once. "La
  dosis hace el veneno."
- **~40% of creative ideas arrive during breaks.** Immersion → incubation →
  intuition. The prescription is the *opposite* of pushing through: when stuck on
  a hard mental task, leave it for at least five minutes; the harder the task,
  the longer the break; for really punishing ones, sleep on it.
- **Walking as the default break.** Six minutes is enough; outdoors is better.
  The book quotes Pressfield's walking-with-a-recorder passage from
  [[la-guerra-del-arte]] — a direct, source-traceable link between two books
  already in this wiki.
- **"Para ser maximalista tienes que ser minimalista"** — decision fatigue as
  the reason to automate trivial choices (clothes, meals, when to train).

**Reinforcement of what was already here** (independent of Magness's own later
book):

- Single-tasking, phone-in-another-room, quality over quantity → [[deep-work]],
  [[minimalismo-digital]]. The phone finding is stronger than Newport's framing:
  phones distract **on or off, in your pocket or on the table, even when they're
  not yours**.
- "No nos volvemos adictos a ganar; nos volvemos adictos a perseguir algo" →
  the pursuit-not-reward mechanism in [[attention-economy-and-pleasure-pain]].
- Ritual, fixed context, portable routines, "la constancia es la reina" →
  [[systems-over-willpower]].
- Just-manageable challenges → [[growth-vs-fixed-mindset]]'s stretch zone and
  Ericsson's deliberate practice (the book explicitly **rejects** Gladwell's
  10,000 hours: "no es la práctica lo que hace la perfección, sino la práctica
  perfecta").

## Tension worth recording

Stulberg & Magness treat **willpower/self-control as a single depletable pool**
shared across unrelated tasks ("tenemos un único recipiente de poder cerebral")
— classic ego-depletion. That literature failed to replicate well after 2016,
which the 2017 book doesn't reflect. Recording it as **stated by the source,
not endorsed**: the *practical* advice it justifies (take on few challenges at
once, design your environment for when you're spent) is supported independently
by [[systems-over-willpower]], so the advice survives even if the mechanism
doesn't. Flagged so a future ingest that contradicts ego-depletion has
something to attach to.

## Where it lands for the user

- **[[absa-garcia]] / running:** the load-recovery architecture is directly
  applicable to the medio maratón arc — and it names the exact failure mode
  already flagged there (training extra to get content). "Un día de descanso
  bien programado aporta enormes dividendos."
- **[[absadev]] / content:** 50–90 min recording/editing blocks after periods
  of intensity; and *dar es un antídoto contra el agotamiento* — teaching
  publicly as burnout prevention is a direct argument for the content work
  itself, not just an audience play. See [[estrategia-contenido-absadev]].
- **[[athletix-ai]]:** the most on-domain book in the wiki alongside
  [[haz-cosas-dificiles]] — load management, overtraining, recovery as trainable
  variables. A **lens on the problem space**, not a product claim.
