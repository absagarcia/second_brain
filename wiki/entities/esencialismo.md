# Esencialismo (Essentialism: The Disciplined Pursuit of Less)

Widely known to be by Greg McKeown (2014) — same caveat as the other book
ingests: not stated in the raw file itself (a Kindle highlights export,
no metadata page), so this attribution is recalled general knowledge, not
source-traceable.

**Nature of this source**: personal Kindle highlights export — same
double-compression caveat as the other book pages in this wiki. Coverage
is uneven across the book's four parts (Define/Explore/Eliminate/Execute)
— some chapters (10, 12-18) appear only as bookmarks with no highlighted
text, so this page doesn't claim to cover the whole book.

## Core thesis

"Menos pero mejor" (less but better) — explicitly not about doing less
for its own sake, nor about doing more things. It's about investing time
and energy as deliberately as possible toward the smallest set of things
that constitute your highest possible contribution. Extracted as its own
concept, see [[essentialism-less-but-better]].

## Structure of the highlighted material

- **Part I (Define)**: choice as a muscle you can forget you have
  ("cuando olvidamos nuestra capacidad de elegir, aprendemos a ser
  indefensos"); most effort doesn't produce proportional results — some
  efforts matter far more than others ("no puedes subestimar la falta de
  importancia de prácticamente todo").
- **Part II (Explore)**: you need unstructured space/time to be able to
  tell the trivial many from the vital few; sleep is reframed as
  protecting your single greatest asset (yourself), not a productivity
  cost, quoting Gandhi's "every night I die, and the next morning I'm
  reborn."
- **Part III (Eliminate)**: the extreme selection criterion — "si no es
  un sí evidente, entonces es un no evidente" (if it isn't a clear yes,
  it's a clear no); clarity about what's essential is what gives you the
  strength to say a graceful no to what isn't.
- **Part IV (Execute)**: closes (in what's highlighted) with a Lao Tzu
  quote on doing what you enjoy in work and being fully present in family
  life.
