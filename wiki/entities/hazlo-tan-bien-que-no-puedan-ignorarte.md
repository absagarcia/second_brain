# Hazlo tan bien que no puedan ignorarte (So Good They Can't Ignore You)

Cal Newport (recalled attribution) — the third Newport book in the wiki, with
[[deep-work]] and [[minimalismo-digital]]. Its central claim is a deliberate
inversion of the standard career advice. **Long-lived** (principles).

## Thesis: don't follow your passion — build career capital

> "No persigas tus sueños; haz que sean estos los que te sigan a ti... 'tan
> bueno que no puedan ignorarte'." (Steve Martin)

- **"Follow your passion" is bad, even dangerous advice.** Passion is rare and
  mostly *follows* competence rather than preceding it (Wrzesniewski's research:
  the happiest workers are those who became very good at something, not those who
  chased a pre-existing dream). Telling someone to follow their passion "puede
  ser la base de una carrera guiada por la confusión y la angustia."
- **Craftsman mindset over passion mindset.** Stop asking "¿qué me puede aportar
  el mundo?" (passion) and ask "¿qué puedo ofrecerle yo al mundo?" (craftsman).
  The craftsman perspective is *liberating* because it drops the anxious
  question of whether the job is "the right one" and just insists on getting
  very good. "Nadie te debe una gran carrera; debes ganártela."
- **Career capital.** Rare and valuable skills are the currency you trade for
  the things that make work great — and they take time (the "10-year / 10,000-hour
  rule," Ericsson). You need capital *before* you can spend it on autonomy.
- **Control traps.** Seeking control/autonomy without enough career capital
  fails; and even with capital, others will resist you leaving. Litmus test:
  "Haz aquello por lo que la gente esté dispuesta a pagarte." If you can't make
  money at the idea, rethink it.
- **Mission requires a market** ("piensa en pequeño, actúa a lo grande"). A
  compelling mission needs (1) people who spread it and (2) a venue receptive to
  it — an idea that rhymes with [[aarrr-growth-metrics]] (referral) and with
  [[monopoly-vs-competition-zero-to-one]] (start where the interest already is).
- **Autonomy / Competence / Connection** (Self-Determination Theory) are the
  three "nutrients" of intrinsic motivation at work.

## Cross-references

- Same author, same intensity-over-volume worldview as [[deep-work]]: deep work
  is *how* you build career capital.
- Contrast recorded with [[la-guerra-del-arte]]: Pressfield says find the calling
  you already are; Newport says the calling is mostly a myth and skill comes
  first. A genuine difference in premise, kept on both pages.
- Reinforces the "work well matters more than having a good job" spirit while
  sitting *against* [[habitos-para-ser-millonario]]'s hustle framing on a
  different axis than the goals-vs-systems one.
