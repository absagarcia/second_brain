# Sin Esfuerzo (Effortless: Make It Easier to Do What Matters Most)

Widely known to be by Greg McKeown (2021) — the direct sequel to
[[esencialismo]] (Essentialism, 2014), same author. Same caveat as the
other book ingests: not stated in the raw file itself (a Kindle
highlights export, no metadata page), so this attribution is recalled
general knowledge, not source-traceable.

**Nature of this source**: personal Kindle highlights export — same
double-compression caveat as the other book pages in this wiki. Broad
coverage across all three parts (Effortless State, Effortless Action,
Effortless Result).

## Explicit relationship to Essentialism

The book states its own positioning directly: **"Esencialismo se trata de
hacer las cosas correctas, Sin Esfuerzo se trata de hacerlas de la manera
correcta"** (Essentialism is about doing the right things; Effortless is
about doing them the right way) — i.e. this book assumes you've already
identified the vital few things (per [[esencialismo]]) and is about
removing unnecessary difficulty from doing them. Extracted as its own
concept given the volume of practical technique, see
[[effortless-action-principles]].

## Key ideas beyond the extracted concept

- **Invert the problem**: "what if this could be easy?" as a deliberate
  reset question when a strategy feels like pushing a load uphill — ask
  for the simplest way to get the result instead of grinding harder.
- **Rituals with soul**: "los rituales son hábitos con alma" — rituals
  are habits we've imprinted with personal meaning, a slightly different
  angle than [[four-laws-of-behavior-change]]'s mechanical "make it
  easy/attractive" framing. Consolidated alongside five other independent
  sources in [[systems-over-willpower]].
- **Rest reframed**: work mornings in three ~90-minute sessions with
  10-15 minute breaks between; naps explicitly cited as counteracting
  sleep debt (research-backed per the excerpt, study not named); guilt
  around napping instead of "getting things done" named directly —
  reinforcing [[esencialismo]]'s "sleep as an asset" and
  [[deep-work]]'s focus-in-bounded-sessions structure, from a third
  independent source.
- **Startup mythology pushback**: explicitly criticizes the "caffeine-
  fueled founders coding in a trance for days until they emerge pale and
  sleep-deprived with their billion-dollar idea" narrative — a direct,
  named critique of hustle-culture startup mythology, reinforcing
  [[founder-mental-health-startup-risk]] and contrasting with
  [[habitos-para-ser-millonario]]'s hustle framing from yet another
  independent source.
- **Attention**: "listening isn't hard, stopping your mind from wandering
  is; being present isn't hard, not thinking about past/future is" —
  reframes several "hard" things as actually being about a single
  underlying difficulty (a wandering mind), not the activity itself.
