## What the book argues

A startup/app-building playbook structured in five escalating "steps"
(Million-Dollar App → Ten-Million → Hundred-Million → Five-Hundred-Million
→ Billion-Dollar App), drawn from interviews with founders of billion-dollar
consumer apps (Facebook, Snapchat, Waze, Hailo are referenced).

Recurring themes across the highlighted passages:

- **Idea validation first.** Build something people are happy to pay for;
  look for problems that are inherently social or can be made social.
- **Three founding roles.** Someone owns product vision, someone builds the
  tech, someone drives users/revenue.
- **Naming is not a blocker.** Don't let naming delay building; short,
  catchy, action-oriented names work best (Hailo, Snapchat, Waze cited as
  examples).
- **Growth metrics.** The book's framework for this is the AARRR funnel and
  the viral coefficient — see [[aarrr-growth-metrics]].
- **Culture/execution mantras.** "Move fast and break things" (Facebook),
  "Done is better than perfect," "Build something users love, and spend
  less than you make."
- **Fundraising is a means, not a goal.** Investment should expedite an
  already-sustainable business plan, not substitute for one.
- **Long odds, framed deliberately.** Cites "0.07% of funded startups become
  billion-dollar companies" — used rhetorically to set expectations, not as
  a rigorously sourced statistic (no methodology given in the excerpt).
