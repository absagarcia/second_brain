# Hábitos para ser millonario

Likely a Spanish edition of Brian Tracy's "Million Dollar Habits" — same
caveat as the other book ingests: not stated in the raw file (a Kindle
highlights export, no metadata), so this is recalled general knowledge,
not source-traceable. Confidence marked low here specifically because,
unlike [[how-to-build-a-billion-dollar-app]], I'm less certain of this
exact title/edition match — ask the user to confirm if it matters.

**Nature of this source**: personal Kindle highlights export, same
double-compression caveat as the other book pages in this wiki (first the
user's highlighting choices, then this summary).

## What the highlighted passages cover

A generic self-help/productivity playbook, structured around daily
habits rather than a single argument. Recurring threads across the
highlights:

- **Definition of success** (intro): the ability to live life the way you
  want, doing what you love, surrounded by people you admire and respect —
  notably not defined in terms of net worth despite the book's title.
- **Goal-setting ritual**: write your goals daily, in the present tense
  ("Mis objetivos son los siguientes:"); write ten goals for the coming
  year in present tense once a year. Framed as building "momentum" through
  daily repeated action toward the goal.
- **Self-talk/optimism habit**: repeating "puedo hacer cualquier cosa que
  me proponga" (I can do anything I set my mind to) as the single most
  important habit for happiness and success, per the book.
- **Frugality rule**: "No malgastes el dinero. Si alguna vez tienes la
  tentación, vuelve a la regla número uno" — rich people reportedly spend
  more time thinking about their finances than poor people do (no
  citation given for this claim). Buy secondhand when possible.
- **Time management / focus**: five questions to stay focused at work
  (why am I on payroll? what are my highest-value activities? can only I
  do this? what delivers the most value to the company?); pick the single
  most important task and work it to completion before switching;
  citation to Robert Half International that the average employee is
  only productive ~50% of the workday (source/study not detailed in the
  excerpt).
- **Work-ethic mantra**: "Repítete constantemente: ¡Vuelve al trabajo!" —
  see the tension noted below.
- Loyalty, forgiveness, and "give without expecting anything back" as
  supporting virtues (Ch. 10-12), plus an early-riser platitude ("el
  hombre que se acuesta y se levanta temprano, es rico, sabio y sano").

## Tensions worth flagging

This book's repeated "back to work" / relentless-productivity framing sits
in tension with [[founder-mental-health-startup-risk]] (extracted from
[[creando-unicornios]]), which explicitly warns founders against exactly
that pattern — over-identifying with work/the company at the cost of
mental health. Both are noted here rather than resolved: they're different
books, different genres (general productivity self-help vs. founder
retrospectives), and the contradiction itself is worth keeping visible
rather than silently picking a side. See the cross-reference added to that
page.

Its daily-written-goals ritual also contrasts with
[[habitos-atomicos]]'s systems-over-goals thesis — see
[[goal-setting-vs-systems-based-habits]] for that comparison.

Its volume-based "more goals, more hustle" approach also sits opposite
[[esencialismo]]'s "menos pero mejor" (less but better) — see
[[essentialism-less-but-better]].

Its frugality rule ("no malgastes el dinero") is independently reinforced
by [[dinero-domina-el-juego]]'s "ganar más, gastar menos y automatizar el
ahorro" — see that page and
[[goal-setting-vs-systems-based-habits]] for how the "automate" part
actually lands closer to Clear's systems side than to this book's own
goal-repetition ritual.

Its "vuelve al trabajo" mantra and citation of ~50%-productive workdays
also sits opposite [[deep-work]]'s explicit naming of "busyness as proxy
for productivity" as a trap — Newport's argument is that more hours of
shallow, distracted work isn't the fix, whereas this book frames the
solution as returning to work more insistently. Recorded as a fourth
independent tension in this cluster, not resolved.

Its "vuelve al trabajo" mantra is also directly opposite
[[sin-esfuerzo]]'s explicit rejection of "caffeine-fueled founder in a
sleep-deprived coding trance" as a mythology worth rejecting rather than
admiring — a fifth independent tension in the same direction, and
[[the-saas-playbook]]'s "founder burnout kills more bootstrapped
businesses than lack of money" — a sixth.

## Retrospective (2026-07-16): how much of this still holds up

Per the [2026-07-16 lint report](../reports/2026-07-16.md), six
independent books ingested into this wiki now push back on parts of this
book's advice. Per this wiki's epistemic-integrity rules, none of the
original content above is deleted or rewritten — this section adds later
context, it doesn't erase what the book said or when it was ingested.

- **Contested**: the "vuelve al trabajo" work-ethic mantra and the
  underlying premise that more hours/more insistence is the fix for low
  productivity. Opposed by [[deep-work]] (busyness ≠ productivity),
  [[sin-esfuerzo]] (rejects the hustle-founder mythology outright),
  [[founder-mental-health-startup-risk]] (multiple unicorn founders in
  [[creando-unicornios]]), and [[the-saas-playbook]] (burnout, not lack
  of money, kills bootstrapped businesses). Four independent sources is a
  strong pattern — worth treating this specific piece of advice with real
  skepticism.
- **Contested**: the daily-written-goals ritual as the primary driver of
  behavior. Opposed by [[habitos-atomicos]]'s systems-over-goals thesis
  (see [[goal-setting-vs-systems-based-habits]]) and by
  [[esencialismo]]'s "menos pero mejor" (volume vs. selectivity). Two
  independent sources, plus this book's own self-talk/affirmation habit
  sits opposite [[systems-over-willpower]]'s six-source convergence on
  environment/system design over repeated self-talk.
- **Independently reinforced, not contested**: the frugality rule ("no
  malgastes el dinero") — corroborated by [[dinero-domina-el-juego]]'s
  "gana más, gasta menos, automatiza el ahorro" from an unrelated author
  and genre. This part of the book's advice has actual independent
  support, unlike the two contested threads above.
- **Not evaluated either way**: the five focus questions ("¿por qué estoy
  en nómina?" etc.), the definition of success in the intro, and the
  loyalty/forgiveness chapters — no other ingested source speaks to these
  directly, so they're neither confirmed nor contradicted by anything
  else in this wiki yet.

This is a synthesis of what other sources in this wiki say, not an
independent judgment of what's actually true — none of the six opposing
books are peer-reviewed either, they're just more numerous and more
specific on these two points than this book is.
