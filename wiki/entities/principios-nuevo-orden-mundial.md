# Principios para enfrentarse al nuevo orden mundial

Ray Dalio (recalled attribution), ~2021. A macro-history thesis: empires,
currencies and orders rise and fall in measurable **cycles**, and studying those
patterns lets you anticipate change instead of being blindsided by it. The
highlights concentrate on Part I (the framework) and the closing principles; the
500-year case studies (Dutch/British/US) are named but not detailed here.
**Mixed lifespan**: the cyclical framework is long-lived; any read on *where we
are now* in the cycle is short-lived and not something to trust years later.

## The Big Cycle

- **Oscillation is the norm, not the exception.** History swings between (1)
  peaceful, prosperous, creative periods that raise living standards and (2)
  depression / revolution / war over power and wealth. Because peaks and troughs
  are decades apart, "los grandes períodos de auge y... declive ocurren una vez
  en la vida y, por tanto, son muy sorprendentes."
- **No system is permanent.** No government, economy, currency, or empire lasts
  forever — yet when one collapses "casi todo el mundo se sorprende y se arruina."
- Three interacting cycles: the long-term **debt/capital-markets** cycle, the
  **internal order/disorder** cycle, and the **external order/disorder** cycle.
- **Productivity is the deepest driver** of rising wealth over time; debt cycles
  and conflict oscillate around that rising line.
- **Reserve-currency & debt dynamics.** Rich empires borrow from poorer, higher-
  saving countries; when debts grow too large the empire can no longer borrow to
  service them, triggering internal strife over a shrinking pie. Central banks can
  "feed" debt by printing money — which erodes the currency.
- **Resilience rule.** Countries (and by analogy, people) with **high savings,
  low debt, and a strong reserve of value** weather collapses far better than the
  over-indebted. Downturns historically last ~3 years.
- **"La historia no se repite, pero rima."**

## Closing personal principles

- **Prioritize long-term over short-term gratification** to be sure you're better
  off in the future.
- **"El conejo inteligente tiene tres madrigueras"** — diversify / keep options.
- **Move among the smartest people you can.** (Same instinct as Hill's
  "mastermind" in [[piense-y-hagase-rico]].)
- "No es tanto lo que sabemos, sino cómo actuamos ante lo que no sabemos."
