# Deep Work

Widely known to be by Cal Newport (2016) — same caveat as the other book
ingests: not stated in the raw file itself (a Kindle highlights export, no
metadata page), so this attribution is recalled general knowledge, not
source-traceable.

**Nature of this source**: personal Kindle highlights export — same
double-compression caveat as the other book pages in this wiki. Coverage
spans the Introduction, Part 1 (chapters 1-3), and Part 2 (Rules 1-4),
denser toward the start.

## Core definitions

- **Deep work**: "professional activities performed in a state of
  distraction-free concentration that push your cognitive capabilities to
  their limit. These efforts create new value, improve your skill, and
  are hard to replicate."
- **The Deep Work Hypothesis**: the ability to do deep work is becoming
  simultaneously rarer and more valuable in the economy — those who
  cultivate it and center their working life on it will thrive.
- **Formula stated explicitly**: High-Quality Work Produced = (Time Spent)
  × (Intensity of Focus). 3-4 hours a day of genuinely uninterrupted,
  directed concentration is framed as enough to produce substantial
  valuable output.
- **Busyness as Proxy for Productivity**: in the absence of clear
  productivity metrics, knowledge workers default to visible busyness
  (doing lots of stuff, visibly) as a stand-in for actual productivity —
  named explicitly as a trap.

## The four rules (Part 2, as highlighted)

1. **Work deeply** — relying on willpower alone to start deep work
   sessions fails often; smart routines/rituals (a fixed time, a quiet
   location, a specific starting action like making coffee) reduce the
   willpower required. Sometimes this means deliberately changing
   environment entirely (an "exotic" location, a week off just to think,
   locking yourself away until a specific piece of work is done).
2. **Embrace boredom** — reframed as: don't take breaks from focus to
   indulge in distraction; instead take scheduled breaks *from* focus,
   keeping distraction-seeking itself rare.
3. **Quit social media** — evaluated via two questions: would the last 30
   days have been notably better with this service? did people actually
   care that you weren't using it? Newport frames the discomfort of
   elimination ("what if I need this one day?") as the real obstacle, not
   the tool's actual value.
4. **Drain the shallows** — treating your time with explicit respect by
   continually asking "what's the best use of the time that remains
   today?" — framed as a habit, not a one-time audit.

## Reinforcements found elsewhere in this wiki

- Rituals reducing reliance on willpower is essentially Newport's version
  of [[four-laws-of-behavior-change]]'s "make it obvious/easy" — a fixed
  deep-work ritual is a concrete instance of environment design over
  willpower. Consolidated with five other independent sources on this
  exact point in [[systems-over-willpower]].
- "A deep life is a good life" and the emphasis on *quality/intensity of
  focus* over volume of hours reinforces [[esencialismo]]'s "menos pero
  mejor" from an independent source — see
  [[essentialism-less-but-better]].
- **Busyness as Proxy for Productivity** sits in direct tension with
  [[habitos-para-ser-millonario]]'s "vuelve al trabajo" mantra and its
  citation that the average employee is only productive ~50% of the
  workday (framed there as a problem to fix with more hours/focus, not a
  wholesale reframing of what productivity even means) — see the note
  added to that page.

- **Extended 2026-07-28 by [[maximo-rendimiento]]**: same prescription (deep
  single-tasking, quality over quantity, phone out of reach), but with two
  additions Newport doesn't make — a **concrete block length** (50–90 min, two
  hours as the ceiling, start at 10–15 min if focus is untrained) and the
  **recovery half of the cycle**, i.e. that the depth of the block is bounded by
  how rested you are. See [[stress-rest-growth-equation]]. Its phone finding is
  also stronger than Newport's: phones degrade attention on **or off**, pocketed
  or on the table, and even when the phone isn't yours.
