# Generación dopamina (Dopamine Nation)

Anna Lembke (recalled attribution), ~2021 — a psychiatrist's account of
compulsion in an age of abundant, on-demand pleasure. **Long-lived** (mechanism
+ practice).

## Core model: the pleasure–pain balance

- The brain processes **pleasure and pain in the same place**, as two sides of a
  balance that always seeks equilibrium (homeostasis). Every pleasure is paid for
  by an opposite, longer, stronger tilt toward pain — "todo placer tiene un
  precio."
- **Dopamine drives the *wanting* more than the *liking*** — motivation to pursue
  a reward, not the pleasure of it.
- **Tolerance & the hedonism→anhedonia paradox.** Chasing pleasure for its own
  sake resets the balance so that ordinary pleasures stop registering; "el
  hedonismo... conduce a la anhedonia." Withdrawal from any compulsion looks the
  same: anxiety, irritability, insomnia, dysphoria.
- We've lost tolerance for even minor discomfort and "buscamos constantemente
  entretenimientos para desviar nuestra atención del momento presente" — the same
  present-moment avoidance [[minimalismo-digital]] blames on the attention economy.

## The practices

- **Dopamine fasting — the DOPAMINE protocol.** Data → Objectives → Problems →
  Abstinence → Mindfulness → Insight → Next → Experiment. Abstinence resets the
  reward pathway so simple pleasures return.
- **Press on the pain side.** Deliberately choosing discomfort (notably
  **exercise**) tips the balance back toward pleasure and *reduces* voluntary drug
  use — replicated even with forced exercise. This is the neurochemical version of
  [[de-que-hablo-cuando-hablo-de-correr]] and [[haz-cosas-dificiles]] (choosing
  the hard thing on purpose) — with the caveat "cuidémonos de volvernos adictos al
  dolor."
- **Radical honesty** creates an abundance mindset; lying creates scarcity.
- **Prosocial shame** binds us to the tribe; social media weaponizes self-shame
  through "tanta comparación odiosa."
