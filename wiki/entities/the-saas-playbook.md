# The SaaS Playbook

Widely known to be by Rob Walling (2023, founder of MicroConf, prominent
bootstrapper) — same caveat as the other book ingests: not stated in the
raw file itself (a Kindle highlights export, no metadata page), so this
attribution is recalled general knowledge, not source-traceable.

**Nature of this source**: personal Kindle highlights export — same
double-compression caveat as the other book pages in this wiki. Dense,
broad coverage: Market, Pricing, Marketing, Team, Metrics, and Mindset
sections.

**Notably the most directly applicable book so far to [[athletix-ai]]'s
actual situation** — a B2B SaaS platform in early stages — more than the
general startup books already in this wiki
([[how-to-build-a-billion-dollar-app]], [[creando-unicornios]],
[[de-cero-a-uno]]), since this one is specifically about bootstrapped
SaaS mechanics rather than VC-backed consumer-app or general startup
narratives.

## Core stance

Explicitly pushes back on "you'll obviously raise VC funding" as the
default startup narrative: "I am not anti-venture-capital, I am
anti-everyone-thinking-venture-capital-is-the-only-way-to-start-a-tech-
company." Advocates building the business first, treating a slide deck as
secondary to an actual working business; bootstrapper's edge: "you don't
need anyone's permission... your business doesn't die until you quit.
Bootstrappers don't run out of money; they run out of motivation."

## Market / product-market fit

- Talk to a spread of people: prospects, customers, people who declined
  to become customers, and people who churned — not just happy customers.
- Discovery questions given concretely (walk me through a sample flow,
  what problem are you solving, what do you currently use, what
  frustrates you about it).
- Cites *Deploy Empathy* by Michele Hansen as a reference for customer
  interviewing.
- "Focus means saying no to the other hundred good ideas" (attributed in
  the excerpt to an unnamed source) — an independent echo of
  [[essentialism-less-but-better]]'s extreme selection criterion, applied
  specifically to product scope.
- **Moat**: for bootstrapped SaaS specifically, the network-effect moat
  comes from integrations, not users — see
  [[network-effects-as-moat]].

## Pricing

- "If no one's complaining about your price, you're probably priced too
  low."
- Freemium only makes sense if free users meaningfully drive paid-tier
  growth.
- "Rob's Rule of 10": raising prices for existing customers is rarely
  worth it unless it grows MRR by at least ~10%.
- Underpricing is framed as mostly psychological (fear of rejection,
  anchoring on cheap competitors) rather than a logistics problem.

## Marketing, team, and metrics

- Founders need at least baseline marketing skill or risk losing
  visibility into a core part of the business — "how you sell is as
  important as what you sell."
- Title convention: "Manager" at the end of a title manages a process,
  not people; at the beginning, manages people.
- Coding is deep work requiring real flow-state focus — an independent,
  unattributed nod to the same idea as [[deep-work]].
- **80/20 metrics**: CAC (cost to acquire a customer); ACV recommended
  over LTV as the key metric to track; expansion revenue (existing
  customers paying more as they get more value) — extends
  [[aarrr-growth-metrics]]'s funnel-stage view with SaaS-specific
  financial metrics.

## Mindset and founder well-being

- "Success comes down to three factors: hard work, luck, and skill,"
  achieved via different personal styles (some high-intensity, some
  steady/purposeful) but sharing common traits.
- Distinguishes "efficient" (getting a lot done) from "effective" (doing
  the things that actually matter) — comparable to
  [[essentialism-less-but-better]] and [[deep-work]]'s intensity-over-
  volume framing.
- On raising funding: money "saves you years" in a business, mainly by
  letting a founder go full-time — framed as a pragmatic tool, not a
  narrative requirement (consistent with the book's core stance above).
- **Founder burnout** named explicitly as a bigger killer of bootstrapped
  businesses than running out of money — directly reinforces
  [[founder-mental-health-startup-risk]] from an independent source, and
  recommends noticing burnout early enough to change habits/patterns
  (including stepping away for weeks) before it compounds, plus finding a
  mentor and considering structured founder retreats.

## Relevance to this wiki

The most operationally relevant book yet for [[athletix-ai]]: the
bootstrapped-SaaS moat framing (integrations), the PMF interview
questions, and the CAC/ACV/expansion-revenue metrics are all directly
applicable lenses for a B2B SaaS platform at ATHLETIX AI's stage — not
asserted as adopted practice, just the most relevant match found so far
among this wiki's book ingests.
