# Sapiens. De animales a dioses (Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind)

Widely known to be by Yuval Noah Harari (2011) — same caveat as the other
book ingests: not stated in the raw file itself (a Kindle highlights
export, no metadata page), so this attribution is recalled general
knowledge, not source-traceable. This is a second, separate book from
[[21-lecciones-siglo-xxi]] by the same author, ingested independently.

**Nature of this source**: personal Kindle highlights export — same
double-compression caveat as the other book pages in this wiki. Coverage
spans the Cognitive, Agricultural, and Scientific Revolutions plus the
Unification of Humankind section, denser in the "imagined orders" chapter
than elsewhere.

## Other notable material in the highlights

- **"The biggest fraud in history" (agricultural revolution chapter)**:
  luxuries reliably become necessities and generate new obligations —
  illustrated with new graduates who tell themselves they'll grind
  through demanding jobs until 35 and then retire into their real
  interests, but by then have mortgages, kids, multiple cars, and can't
  imagine life without expensive wine and vacations, so they keep
  working instead. Directly comparable to
  [[habitos-para-ser-millonario]]'s frugality rule and
  [[esencialismo]]/[[essentialism-less-but-better]]'s "less but better" —
  from a historical-anthropological angle rather than a
  personal-finance-advice angle.
- **Memory overload → writing**: the human brain is described as a poor
  database for empire-scale information for three reasons — limited
  capacity even for skilled mnemonists, information dies with the brain
  (and degrades across transmissions even before that), and the brain
  evolved to store specific kinds of information (e.g. species/behavior
  patterns for hunter-gatherers), not arbitrary administrative data. This
  is presented as the actual origin pressure behind writing (Sumerian
  administrative record-keeping).
- **Religion as syncretism**: "syncretism might really be the one great
  world religion." Also covers the Buddha's account of suffering as
  caused by patterns of mind rather than misfortune or divine whim, and
  "craving" (not wanting itself) as the root of suffering — connected to
  a chapter on meditation as observing "what am I experiencing now"
  rather than "what would I rather be experiencing."
- **Capitalism-consumerism as a quasi-religion**: "invest!" as the
  commandment for the rich, "buy!" as the commandment for everyone else —
  described as the first belief system in history whose followers
  actually do what's asked of them, because they've seen the promised
  reward on television.
- **Happiness research**: a psychological "ceiling" (~level 7 happiness)
  argued to cap subjective well-being regardless of external
  circumstances, even hypothetically curing AIDS/cancer and brokering
  world peace in the same day — used to question whether happiness
  research forces an uncomfortable conclusion that contentment depends on
  self-deception. Reinforced from the finance side by
  [[el-arte-de-gastar-dinero]] (added 2026-07-23): Housel's "riqueza = lo
  que tienes menos lo que quieres" and satisfaction-over-happiness-spikes
  are the personal-finance restatement of this ceiling and the
  luxury/necessity ratchet above — both consolidated in
  [[enough-hedonic-adaptation]].
