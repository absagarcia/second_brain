# Piense y hágase rico (Think and Grow Rich)

Napoleon Hill (recalled attribution), ~1937 — one of the founding texts of the
modern success/self-help genre. Dense highlight set. **Confidence set to low
overall**, not because it's obscure but because a large fraction of its content
is dated pseudoscience (thought "magnetism," the "sixth sense," "sex
transmutation," communing with dead geniuses at an imaginary conference table).
Separating the mechanically useful from the mystical is the whole job of this
page. **Mixed lifespan**: the goal-setting/persistence mechanics are long-lived;
the psychology and physiology claims are largely obsolete.

## The parts that still function

- **Definiteness of purpose + the written-goal ritual.** Hill's six steps: fix
  the *exact* amount, decide what you'll give in return, set a deadline, make a
  plan, write it all down, and **read it aloud twice a day (waking and before
  sleep), seeing yourself already in possession.** This is the same daily-goals
  ritual as [[habitos-para-ser-millonario]] — and it lands on the *same contested
  side* of [[goal-setting-vs-systems-based-habits]]. This book is now a **third
  independent instance** of the read-your-goals-aloud ritual in the wiki.
- **Persistence.** "Los que abandonan nunca ganan, y un ganador nunca abandona."
  Persistence formula: definite purpose + definite plan in continuous action + a
  mind closed to discouragement + a supportive alliance.
- **Mastermind ("equipo de trabajo").** Deliberately allying with others whose
  knowledge and drive compound your own — an old statement of the "surround
  yourself with the smartest people" advice that [[principios-nuevo-orden-mundial]]
  (Dalio) also gives.
- **Systematic saving as courage.** "Habitúese a ahorrar sistemáticamente un
  porcentaje... Tener dinero en el Banco da... coraje." Converges with
  [[investment-bucket-strategy]] (automate savings) and [[el-arte-de-gastar-dinero]]
  (independence as wealth).
- **Self-analysis inventory** (28-question annual review) — a structured
  retrospective habit.

## The parts flagged as obsolete / unsupported

- **Thought "magnetism"** attracting like thoughts, and telepathic "vibrations."
- **The "sixth sense"** and the imagined council of dead great men consulted in
  half-sleep.
- **"Sex transmutation"** as the engine of genius, plus the claim that "no great
  leader/builder/artist ever lacked a highly sexual nature," that men rarely
  succeed before 40 because they dissipate this energy, and that great men are
  driven by "el deseo de agradar a la mujer." Presented in 1937 as fact; recorded
  here as period-bound assertion with no evidence, **not** endorsed.

Kept per this wiki's retrospective-integrity rule: the point is not to delete
what a 1937 bestseller claimed, but to mark clearly *which* claims a modern reader
should not carry forward.
