# Career Capital & the Craftsman Mindset

From [[hazlo-tan-bien-que-no-puedan-ignorarte]] (Cal Newport). **Long-lived.**

## The argument

- **"Follow your passion" is bad advice.** Passion is rare and usually *follows*
  mastery rather than preceding it. Prescribing passion-first breeds "una carrera
  guiada por la confusión y la angustia."
- **Craftsman mindset over passion mindset.** Swap "¿qué me puede aportar el
  mundo?" for "¿qué puedo ofrecerle yo al mundo?". Focusing on what the work gives
  *you* breeds chronic dissatisfaction; focusing on getting very good is
  liberating.
- **Career capital** = rare and valuable skills, the currency you later trade for
  autonomy, mission, and impact. It takes ~10 years / 10,000 hours (Ericsson), and
  you must earn it *before* spending it.
- **Control traps**: autonomy sought without enough capital collapses; and once
  you have capital, others resist your using it. Litmus test — "haz aquello por lo
  que la gente esté dispuesta a pagarte."
- **A mission needs a market**: it must be spread by people and land in a
  receptive venue — cf. [[aarrr-growth-metrics]] (referral) and
  [[monopoly-vs-competition-zero-to-one]] (go where interest already is).

## Tensions worth keeping

- Against [[la-guerra-del-arte]]: Pressfield says find the calling you already
  *are*; Newport says the calling is mostly a myth and skill comes first.
- Against [[elon-musk]]'s "hire for attitude, skills can be learned": Newport says
  skill *is* the scarce asset. Different vantage points (hiring vs. building a
  career), recorded, not resolved.
- Complements rather than contradicts [[growth-vs-fixed-mindset]]: both say
  ability is built by deliberate work, not discovered.
