# Falacia narrativa, confirmación y pruebas silenciosas

The three belief-formation failures Taleb bundles in Part I of
[[el-cisne-negro]]. Grouped on one page because they are a single machine: we
generate a story, we hunt for what confirms it, and we never see what the story
excludes. **Long-lived** (cognitive mechanics).

## 1. Falacia narrativa

> "Nuestra mente es una magnífica máquina de explicación, capaz de dar sentido a
> casi todo… y generalmente incapaz de aceptar la idea de la impredecibilidad."

Explanation is compression, and compression is where the randomness gets thrown
away. The lottery version: players "sobreestiman las probabilidades… porque
visualizan las grandes cantidades de dinero"; they "tratan casi del mismo modo
la de una entre mil que la de una entre un millón" — a vivid story flattens two
probabilities three orders of magnitude apart.

**This is the direct threat to a second brain.** Every summary written into
`wiki/` is a narrative imposed on a source, which is why
[[second-brain-epistemic-design]] requires stating the compressor's lens. Taleb
supplies the mechanism behind that rule from outside the PKM literature.

## 2. Error de confirmación

The asymmetry, in Taleb's compressed form: "«la mayoría de los asesinos son
animales salvajes»" does **not** license "«la mayoría de los animales salvajes
son asesinos»." Corroboration is not proof; a thousand confirming days say
nothing about day 1001 (the turkey — see [[limites-de-la-prediccion-experta]]).

Umberto Eco's **antilibrary** (Part I's title) is the counter-practice: the
value of a library is the unread books, the "libros no leídos" that Taleb
returns to in ch. 10 as the content of the unknown unknowns. A reading list is
an inventory of what you don't know, not a trophy shelf — a useful correction to
how a `books`-heavy wiki like this one is tempted to read its own index.

## 3. Pruebas silenciosas (survivorship bias)

> "Las estadísticas son invisibles; las anécdotas destacan. Asimismo, el riesgo
> de un Cisne Negro es invisible."

The drowned worshippers never testify. Taleb's two worked examples in these
highlights:

- **Casanova**'s indestructible luck reads as skill only because the equally
  bold men who died young wrote no memoirs.
- **The Nobel bookshelf** (ch. 14) — Sully Prudhomme, Pearl Buck, Anatole
  France, Romain Rolland — a "definitive library" assembled by a mechanical
  criterion, now unread. The selection process left no visible record of what it
  wrongly excluded.

Same shape as "«coger de Pedro para dárselo a Pablo»": a visible benefit whose
invisible cost lands on someone unmeasured.

## 4. Falacia lúdica

Casino uncertainty — bounded, rule-specified, computable — mistaken for
real-world uncertainty. The gambler's real risk is not the dice but the kidnap
of the casino owner's daughter. Related maxim from the trading floor: "«descubre
quién es el idiota»" — if you can't tell, it's you.

## Where this bites in this wiki

- **Any post-hoc explanation of a metric spike.** The World Cup surge in
  [[absa-garcia]] and the DM-driven batch #5 in [[estrategia-contenido-absadev]]
  both invite a clean causal story. The honest version keeps the sample size and
  the alternatives visible.
- **Founder/creator success stories.** [[creando-unicornios]] and
  [[how-to-build-a-billion-dollar-app]] are, structurally, collections of
  survivors. [[founder-mental-health-startup-risk]] partially escapes this
  because it draws on failures too ([[casai]]).
- **Book advice generally.** [[piense-y-hagase-rico]] is already flagged in this
  wiki as dated pseudoscience; silent evidence names *why* that genre
  self-perpetuates — the people who followed the advice and failed didn't write
  the sequel.
- **ML/AI evaluation (`masters`).** Confirmation-seeking on a benchmark and
  invisible negative results are the same failure in lab clothes.
