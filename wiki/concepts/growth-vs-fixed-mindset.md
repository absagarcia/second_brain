# Growth vs. Fixed Mindset

**Long-lived framework**: Carol Dweck's core distinction in
[[mindset-la-actitud-del-exito]] — a psychological model, not tied to any
market/data point, so it doesn't decay the way a stat would.

## The two mindsets

- **Fixed mindset**: belief that your basic qualities (intelligence,
  talent, character) are fixed traits. Success becomes about *proving*
  how smart/talented you already are — "the world of self-affirmation."
  Failure threatens self-worth directly, which leads to avoidance,
  excuse-making, or blaming others rather than engaging with the failure.
- **Growth mindset**: belief that basic qualities can be cultivated
  through effort, regardless of starting differences in talent, interest,
  or temperament. Success becomes about *stretching yourself* to learn
  something new — "the world of self-development." The defining trait is
  passion for challenge and a drive toward self-improvement especially
  when things go badly, not just when they go well.

## Practical mechanics

- **"Not yet"**: reframing failure/incompletion (e.g. a failing grade) as
  "not yet" rather than a fixed verdict — treating competence as a
  trajectory, not a snapshot.
- **Failure doesn't define you**: it's a problem to engage with, struggle
  against, and learn from — not an identity marker.
- **Plans need to be concrete** (when, where, how), not just a stated
  intention — abstract goals rarely convert into sustained action.
- **Willpower needs external help/structure** — it isn't a fixed
  personal trait either.
- Applies across domains: sports ("character"), business
  leadership/culture, romantic relationships, and how parents/coaches/
  teachers phrase praise and feedback to children (praising the process
  and learning, not fixed traits).

## Relevance to this wiki

- [[sin-esfuerzo]]'s "coraje de ser basura" (the courage to be bad at
  first — "no mastery without mistakes") is a direct practical
  instance of growth-mindset thinking, ingested independently and
  cross-linked here.
- Complements [[four-laws-of-behavior-change]]'s identity-based habits:
  Clear's model is about deciding who you want to be and proving it
  through action; Dweck's is about believing that "who you are" (in terms
  of ability) is itself changeable through that same action. Different
  emphasis (identity formation vs. belief about capability), same
  underlying mechanism (repeated action changes self-concept).
- Independently reinforces the "willpower needs structure/help" thread
  also present in [[deep-work]] and [[four-laws-of-behavior-change]].
