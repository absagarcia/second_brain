# Intelligence as Goal-Achieving Capacity

**Long-lived definition**: this is a standard, still-current working
definition used broadly in AI/AGI discussions (Tegmark attributes a
version of it to a broader consensus-seeking exercise among AI
researchers, though that provenance detail isn't present in this book's
excerpt — treat the attribution as coming from [[vida-3-0]] only).

## The definition

"Intelligencia = capacidad de alcanzar objetivos complejos" — intelligence
as the capacity to achieve complex goals, independent of the substrate
(biological or artificial) doing the achieving.

## Why it's useful

- It's substrate-agnostic: it doesn't presuppose consciousness, biological
  neurons, or any particular architecture — just goal-achievement capacity
  across a range of complex objectives.
- It sidesteps the temptation to define intelligence by proxy (test
  scores, specific tasks) in favor of a general capacity framing.

## Relevance to this wiki

Useful reference definition for `masters` coursework/notes on AI. Loosely
adjacent to [[pattern-recognition-professions]] (which frames expertise as
pattern recognition rather than goal-achievement) — worth comparing the
two framings if this area gets developed further, but no direct
contradiction or dependency between them yet.
