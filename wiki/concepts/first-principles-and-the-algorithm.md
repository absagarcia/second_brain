# First-Principles Thinking & "The Algorithm"

From [[elon-musk]] (Isaacson). Two linked operating ideas, useful as an
engineering/product discipline. **Long-lived.**

## First principles

Reason from what physics allows, not from analogy or precedent. "Si el
pensamiento convencional hace que tu misión sea imposible... piensa más allá de
lo convencional." The only hard constraints are physical laws; everything else
("el departamento legal dice…") is a *recommendation* that must carry a named
owner and be open to challenge — "las especificaciones indicadas por las personas
inteligentes son las más peligrosas."

## The Algorithm (fixed order)

1. **Question every requirement** (name-owned, then make it less dumb).
2. **Delete** parts/steps — if you don't later re-add ~10%, you didn't delete
   enough.
3. **Simplify & optimize** — only *after* deleting, or you polish what shouldn't
   exist.
4. **Accelerate** cycle time.
5. **Automate** — last.

The load-bearing insight is the **ordering**: deletion precedes optimization, and
automation comes dead last. The Nevada/Fremont lesson was automating a process
that should have been removed.

## Why it sits in this wiki's productivity cluster

The delete-before-optimize rule is the manufacturing statement of the same
principle three other sources reach from softer directions:

- [[esencialismo]] — "less but better," extreme selection: remove the
  nonessential *first*.
- [[effortless-action-principles]] / [[sin-esfuerzo]] — simplify to the minimum
  viable step.
- [[typescript-5-design-patterns]] — the engineering echo: don't add a pattern's
  abstraction until its criteria are genuinely met; every pattern chapter ends on
  "over-engineering" as the failure mode.

Recorded as convergence across independent sources, not shared lineage. Where the
McKeown books argue it as *life* discipline, Musk operationalizes it as a factory
algorithm with a strict sequence — the added value here is the *order*.
