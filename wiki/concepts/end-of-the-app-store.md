# End of the App Store

Thesis discussed with [[daniel]] (opinion/speculation, not a sourced
claim — long-lived idea but unvalidated): once app generation becomes
free and instant via LLMs, the App Store's core value proposition
(exclusive distribution) collapses, because anyone can regenerate a
copied app on demand instead of needing to buy/discover it in a store.

Supporting analogies raised in the conversation:
- **Sideloaded/self-updating app cores**: old iOS enterprise-style apps
  that shipped their own update mechanism outside the App Store, and the
  "app of apps" model (compared to old Nintendo cartridges that bundled
  many mini-games behind one "shell" purchase).
- **Superapps** (WeChat-style): one app acts as its own internal app
  store, sidestepping the platform store entirely.
- **Browser tooling lineage**: bookmarklets → Tampermonkey scripts →
  browser extensions — cited as a precedent for how personal, ad hoc
  automation tools get gradually productized and generalized.

Counter-point (also raised, and the more durable part of the thesis):
**network effects survive** even if generation is free. Marketplaces like
Airbnb, eBay, or attention-aggregators like TikTok/Facebook keep value
not from the app itself (trivially recreatable) but from already having
"all the eyes" — the two-sided market/audience is the moat, not the
software.

Related shift: TikTok's value was reframed in the conversation as having
moved from *content creation* (scarce when cameras/internet were rare) to
*selection/curation* (the recommendation algorithm), as content-generation
cost keeps falling (AI-generated music etc.) — paralleled to software,
where the same shift from "building the app" to "selecting/generating the
right features for you" is argued to be underway.
