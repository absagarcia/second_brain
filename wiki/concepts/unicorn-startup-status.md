# Unicorn (Startup Valuation Status)

**Long-lived definition, short-lived examples**: the term and its
$1B-valuation threshold are stable and well established; any specific list
of "current unicorns" or company valuations decays fast and should be
re-verified rather than trusted from this page.

## Definition

A privately held startup that reaches a valuation of USD 1 billion or more
while still in its market-launch/growth stage. Per [[creando-unicornios]]:

- Technology use is treated as a requirement — either novel products or
  meaningfully better/faster delivery of existing ones.
- Valuations at this stage are explicitly **not** based on real
  calculations — they're estimates based on perceived potential, not
  audited fundamentals. This is a repeated caveat in the source, not
  incidental.
- Reaching unicorn status doesn't necessarily happen quickly — trajectories
  vary widely per company.

## Origin

Coined by Aileen Lee in "Welcome to the Unicorn Club: Learning from
Billion-Dollar Startups" (TechCrunch, 2013). Lee revisited the term a
decade later in "Welcome Back to The Unicorn Club: 10 Years Later" (2024,
per the excerpt — not independently verified here, just as cited by
[[creando-unicornios]]).

## Founder stats commonly cited (source/methodology not specified in the excerpt)

- ~33% of lead unicorn founders have elite-university degrees — used to
  argue this is less common than assumed.
- ~49% are serial entrepreneurs (had prior founding experience).
- ~61% studied CS, electrical engineering, or math.

Treat these as claims to verify independently rather than confirmed data —
no citation trail is given beyond "the book says so."

## Critique / caveat worth keeping

[[creando-unicornios]]'s own conclusion argues valuation is not a proxy for
success: none of the founders profiled were chasing the label itself, and
becoming a unicorn early "podría ser una maldición" (could be a curse) —
i.e. premature scale before the business fundamentals are ready.

## Relevance to this wiki

Useful vocabulary/frame when discussing [[athletix-ai]]'s growth
trajectory, but nothing in the ATHLETIX AI pitch-deck ingest frames the
company in "unicorn" terms — this is background context, not something
the company has claimed or targeted.

[[de-cero-a-uno]] pushes this critique further: Peter Thiel's
[[monopoly-vs-competition-zero-to-one]] argues the real marker of success
is having a genuine monopoly position at all, well before any valuation
event — consistent with, and more specific than, this page's point that
valuation isn't a proxy for success.
