# Esfuerzo + descanso = crecimiento

**Long-lived principle.** From [[maximo-rendimiento]] (Stulberg & Magness):
growth is not produced by effort, and not by rest, but by **alternating** them.
"El esfuerzo pide descanso, y el descanso hace posible el esfuerzo." The claim
is that this holds regardless of what you're developing — muscle, a skill, a
business, a channel — because the mechanism is adaptation to a dose, and
**adaptation happens during recovery, not during load**.

The equation is the wiki's first explicit **periodization** frame: most other
productivity sources here optimize the *load* side only. This one says an
unrecovered load produces less growth than a smaller recovered one.

## The load side

- **La dosis hace el veneno.** Stress isn't harmful or useful in itself; the
  effect depends almost entirely on dose. Too little → no adaptation; too much →
  overtraining/burnout (30–40% of college and amateur athletes have hit
  overtraining syndrome at least once).
- **Retos apenas manejables** (just-manageable challenges): aim at what sits at
  the edge of, or slightly beyond, current capacity. Diagnostic: if you feel
  fully in control, make the next one harder; if you're anxious or too activated
  to concentrate, ease off. "Una pequeña duda e incertidumbre en realidad es algo
  positivo." Same zone as [[growth-vs-fixed-mindset]]'s stretch and the authentic
  goals-just-beyond-capacity of [[haz-cosas-dificiles]].
- **Falla productivamente.** Struggle first, ask for help only after — the
  effortful attempt is what recruits System 2 and lays down the learning.
- **Práctica perfecta, no 10.000 horas.** The book explicitly separates Ericsson
  from Gladwell's popularization: mastery is about the *type* of work filling
  the hours, not the count. Each session gets a stated purpose and concrete
  objective ("¿qué quiero aprender o realizar?").
- **Unitarea.** Multitasking degrades everything in the stack. Phones distract
  whether on, off, pocketed, or on the table — and even when the phone isn't
  yours. Stronger than the framing in [[deep-work]] / [[minimalismo-digital]].
- **Bloques de 50–90 min**, two hours as the hard ceiling; start at 10–15 min if
  focus is new to you and extend as it builds.
- **Reframe the arousal.** Stress is the body preparing for a challenge, not a
  malfunction — how you read it changes the physiological response. Independent
  of, and consistent with, [[mindset-la-actitud-del-exito]].

## The rest side — the part that gets skipped

**Rest is productive work, not its absence.** Coach Matt Dixon's athletes are
taught to stop reading recovery as "no entrenamiento"; once reframed, it becomes
as productive as an extra session.

- **Short breaks.** When stuck on a hard task, leave it for ≥5 minutes; the more
  stressful the task, the longer the break; for genuinely punishing ones, leave
  it until morning. Fill breaks with low-cognitive-demand activity: walk, sit in
  nature, shower, wash dishes, listen to music.
- **Why it works:** immersion → **incubation** → intuition. ~40% of creative
  ideas surface during breaks. "La única razón para hacer el trabajo es preparar
  el camino a lo que ocurre cuando te alejas de él."
- **Walking** is the cheapest entry to the subconscious — six minutes is enough,
  outdoors preferred. The book quotes Pressfield's pocket-recorder walks from
  [[la-guerra-del-arte]].
- **Meditation** as the trainable capacity to *choose* rest: start at one minute,
  add 30–45 s every few days, frequency over duration.
- **Social recovery.** Being with people at the end of intense work measurably
  shifts the stress physiology.
- **Sleep, hardest-hitting claim in the book.** 7–9 h (10 h isn't excessive under
  heavy physical load); REM lengthens with each cycle, so **hours 7 through 9 are
  the most potent** — precisely the ones people cut. "No es que duerman porque
  sean la élite. Son la élite porque duermen." To find your own number: 10–14
  days going to bed when tired, waking without an alarm, take the average.
- **Days off and vacations, scheduled *after* accumulated stress**, not
  randomly. At least one day a week regardless of the work. "Si nunca nos tomamos
  períodos 'fáciles', nunca seremos capaces de ir a todo gas."
- **Naps** of 10–30 min.

## How this relates to what the wiki already holds

- **Extends, doesn't repeat, [[essentialism-less-but-better]].** McKeown already
  treats sleep as an asset; this adds the *periodization* — not just "protect
  rest" but "place rest immediately after load."
- **Complements [[systems-over-willpower]].** Both say don't rely on in-the-moment
  discipline; this one adds that the discipline required is largely a function of
  how recovered you are.
- **Sits under [[internal-scorecard]]'s ceiling.** Just-manageable challenges are
  calibrated against *your* current capacity, not anyone else's.
- **Not independent of [[haz-cosas-dificiles]]** — same author (Magness). Treated
  here as one voice across two books, not two confirming sources.

## Practical application in the user's context

- **Running / [[absa-garcia]]:** the arc toward the medio maratón is the literal
  domain of this equation; the "don't over-train to produce content" risk already
  flagged there is exactly the dose failure this page describes.
- **Content / [[absadev]]:** batch recording in 50–90 min blocks; schedule the
  down-week *after* a shipping batch rather than pushing straight into the next.
- **[[athletix-ai]]:** load-vs-recovery as trainable, measurable variables — the
  conceptual frame behind injury-prevention products (a lens, not a claim about
  the product).
