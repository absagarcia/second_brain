# Goal-Setting vs. Systems-Based Habit Formation

Two self-help books now in this wiki give genuinely different answers to
"how do you achieve big outcomes day to day?" — recorded here as a
comparison rather than resolved, since neither source cites the other or
any shared evidence base.

## [[habitos-para-ser-millonario]] — write goals daily

- Daily ritual: write your goals in the present tense every morning; once
  a year, write ten goals for the coming year, also in present tense.
- Treats the goal itself, explicitly stated and repeated, as the driver of
  behavior — momentum comes from daily repeated action *toward a named
  target*.
- Pairs this with a self-talk/affirmation habit ("puedo hacer cualquier
  cosa que me proponga") aimed directly at belief.

## [[habitos-atomicos]] — systems over goals, identity over affirmation

- Explicit critique of goal-fixation: "cuando logras alcanzar una meta, tu
  vida cambia únicamente durante un momento" — the paradox of
  improvement. Argues you should redesign the **system** (the process)
  that produces results, not just the target outcome, because fixing the
  system fixes the outcome as a side effect.
- Argues durable change comes from **identity change** (deciding who you
  want to be, then proving it through small wins) rather than from
  repeating a wanted belief to yourself — see
  [[four-laws-of-behavior-change]].

## What's actually in tension

- Written daily goals (Tracy) vs. "goals are only useful to set direction,
  systems are what you actually work on" (Clear) — these are close to
  incompatible as daily practices, though not necessarily mutually
  exclusive (you could set a yearly goal *and* build a system to pursue
  it).
- Direct self-affirmation of belief (Tracy) vs. earning belief through
  repeated small actions (Clear) — different causal direction for the
  same target (self-confidence → future behavior, in either order).

## A concrete example landing on the "systems" side

[[dinero-domina-el-juego]]'s "great secret" — "ganar más, gastar menos y
automatizar el ahorro" (earn more, spend less, **automate** savings) — is
a real-world financial instance of Clear's argument: automating savings
removes reliance on daily willpower or repeated goal-affirmation entirely,
which is closer to "fix the system" than to Tracy's write-your-goals-daily
ritual. Noted here as one more data point on the systems side, from a
third, independent book.

## Relevance to this wiki

No resolution attempted here — these are self-help/personal-finance books
without a shared evidence base, and the point of recording this
comparison is to keep both framings visible rather than silently adopting
one. Worth revisiting if [[athletix-ai]]'s own year-by-year growth targets
ever get evaluated against either framing.
