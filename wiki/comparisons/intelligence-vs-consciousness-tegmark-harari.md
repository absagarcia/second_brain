# Definitions of Intelligence and Consciousness — Tegmark vs. Harari

Two AI-adjacent books in this wiki both offer one-line definitions of
intelligence, from different angles. Recorded as a comparison rather than
merged, since they're not strictly the same claim and neither source
engages with the other.

## [[vida-3-0]] (Max Tegmark)

"Inteligencia = capacidad de alcanzar objetivos complejos" — intelligence
as the capacity to achieve complex goals. Substrate-agnostic (doesn't
presuppose consciousness or biological wetware). See
[[intelligence-as-goal-achieving-capacity]].

## [[21-lecciones-siglo-xxi]] (Yuval Noah Harari)

"La inteligencia es la capacidad de resolver problemas. La conciencia es
la capacidad de sentir dolor, alegría, amor e ira." — intelligence as
problem-solving capacity, explicitly separated from **consciousness**
(the capacity to feel). Harari's framing is doing different work here:
the split from Tegmark's is less about defining intelligence precisely
and more about warning that an entity can be highly intelligent
(problem-solving) without being conscious (feeling) — a distinction
relevant to his broader argument (not present in this sparse excerpt)
about AI systems potentially outperforming humans at tasks without
possessing any inner experience.

## What's actually comparable vs. not

- Both frame intelligence as a **capacity** rather than a fixed trait or
  test score, and both frame it independent of *how* it's implemented
  (biological or artificial) — genuinely convergent.
- Tegmark's definition folds "achieving goals" as the core; Harari's
  folds "solving problems" — close but not identical framings of the same
  underlying idea (goal-achievement typically requires problem-solving,
  but not all problem-solving is goal-directed at Tegmark's scale).
- Harari adds a second term (consciousness) that Tegmark's quoted
  definition doesn't address in this excerpt — not a contradiction, just
  a different scope. Tegmark's book does address consciousness at length
  elsewhere (see [[vida-3-0]]'s own consciousness/meaning highlight), just
  not in the same quoted definition.

## Relevance to this wiki

Useful pairing for `masters` coursework/notes — both are citable,
independent one-liners on the same topic from two widely-read
AI-adjacent books, without requiring either source to be treated as more
authoritative than the other.
